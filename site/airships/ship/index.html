There is no interior. The inside is the product. A vacuum ship has no bay to hide machinery in, and its wall has no spare capacity for a single bolt hole. Every system lives outside, and this level draws that doctrine: thrust pods standing off on pylons longer than their own rotor radius, circumferential straps and twin keel rails as the only interface the gear may have with the wall, and the working machinery (tanks, pumps, and winch) on a raft suspended under the keel from a wide bridle.

The raft is suspended, not bolted to the belly. A hard-mounted gondola feeds its thrust and slosh moments straight into film-on-rings, and the 4 mm wall has no line for that local bending. Pendants on a wide bridle arrive nearly tangential, so the wall sees distributed pulls along straps that ride many rings in bearing. The hull is huge, so the bridle can be wide and the swing angle small. The price is a pendulum in the control loop, and ops owns it. Both readings are [SCOPING]. The equipment axis is scoped rather than weighed in the gated ledger; Mission 0 carries it as a spec figure: 19 t of gear against 100 t of water at 112 m.

The rotors have two jobs, priced worlds apart, and the wash decides where they sit. Cruise thrust is cheap: a trimmed-neutral ship fights only drag. HOLDDOWN is the expensive duty: the scoop-and-drop transient is ~100 t of force. Holddown's wash goes up, because pushing the ship down means throwing air at the sky. A pod mounted low would fire its hardest wash straight into the belly above it. The pods therefore ride on the horizontal plane at the widest band's beam. There, the wash column clears the hull's curve in both duties. The discs sit as far from the wall, the straps and the working lines as a pylon can hold them, and the fore-aft spread buys pitch and roll authority. Every pod gimbals through 90°, and symmetric pairs cancel their own moments. They are drawn mid-duty, pointed to push the ship down and forward at once: the posture the water cycle actually flies. This placement is decided [SCOPING]. Real disc sizing belongs to the fleet-model power work. (The largest class stays on the horizontal line too: four stations at normal reach and three interleaved on extra-long pylons standing wider. Its fourteen discs separate by radius, never by height.)

The working end is two decks. The raft's upper deck carries the single 107 m³ water tank amidships, the Mission-0 100 t with trim margin, with the two N₂ ballast tanks beside it, their bellies on the same deck line as the water tank. EQUIPMENT ON THE LOWER DECK: a see-through equipment bay rides the drop line, batteries boxed along its ceiling, the N₂ cryo unit low on one flank and the SHIP MIND, the compute core in its own pink glass box, on the other, with three pulleys on its keel. The lines wear a colour code: pink carries weight, blue carries water. The SPRAYER is a nozzle on its blue feed line to one side. The BUCKET hangs centred on pink, with clearance to both neighbours. The PUMP hangs from the end of its rigid blue pipe at the winch. The pipe provides both the 100 m reach and the suspension, and the pump is the only piece that works below the surface. Three pulleys, three working lines, and nothing hangs past the water: what holds the ship down against its own buoyancy while it drinks is the rotors throwing their wash upward, not a weight on a cable. The ENTIRE top half of the hull is solar, rectangular panels edge-on to the grid with visible seams; the energy budget lives in the fleet model. Switch on environment to see it working: dipping into a lake with trees and people at the shore. Then fly down and stand with them.

Mission 0 wears this same fit. The spec ship is the reference for every quoted number. It uses this architecture at 112 m, with 100 t of water and 19 t of equipment. It is neutrally buoyant at sea level in the certified world. The 52 m hull comes first; Mission 0 is its next configuration. The hull below carries the whole verdict.

Everything the model prices is on screen. The pressure-formed film over its ring grid; the cross-bars; the inner wall's longerons and rings; the meridional fan; the ring-plane X-webs the checks found missing; the junction diagonals where the domes hand their thrust inward; and the spokes: pretensioned cords across the void, the ruled tension exception, fighting the ovalization. The verdict is a pair. In the friendliest defensible world, frame-practice knockdown with sourced-ceiling coupons, the ship comes within two percent of its own lift. On the house-harsh stability basis it is short at both declared factors. What stands between them is two test campaigns and two design moves the checks named. The moves are a chordal spoke net for the odd out-of-round modes, and an in-surface shear system that buys the membrane term back. Two independent review panels moved this number twice in one night, each time toward the truth. Because the ledger is gated, each move landed on every page at once. None of it is hidden in an average.

The wall to beat is kg/m³: the mass of a cubic metre of air at the working altitude. Every level below this one is the fight to get a cubic metre of ship under that number, and the fight is now scored by a gated model, not a hope.

Compartments are the next policy decision. Membranes cross the void so a holed wall floods one room instead of the ship. A cell-sized hole swallows roughly ten tonnes of air a minute. Membranes are already priced as skins in the ledger; the compartments level returns drawn from the decided rule, not from a guess.

The X in the ring plane is a checks finding, drawn. The fan's diagonals all live in meridional planes, so they cannot carry ring-plane shear. Without it, the two walls cannot bend as one deep ring, which is the entire point of keeping them apart. The checks found the gap, priced the fix, and the crossed diagonals at every bay plane are the fix.

A connector at every landing. Web ends on their rings, X ends on theirs, and the crossing itself: the beads are where fittings live. The ledger prices joints as a smeared joint-efficiency allowance, 15% of the members and joints together, 46.5 t at ship scale, rather than as a count. Reducing that count is the work of building.

The film lies on the rings. The ruling survived its own optimisation. A ring loaded by film along its whole length sees uniform radial load, the case a hoop is funicular for. The pitch sweep says the ruled half-metre squares sit within a couple of percent of the optimum: the ruling cost almost nothing.

Square panels, priced the conservative way. The shape. A truncated octahedron fills space with the least wall per unit volume — eight hexagons, six squares, thirty-six edges. Near-spherical, so the buckling radius is the cell's own; space-filling, so cells seat face to face and the array becomes the airframe rather than cargo inside one.

The interior and the boundary do different jobs. The interior is pure compression: one mm cut, repeated 144 times. The boundary is not: the film pulls the edge frame sideways rather than pushing on the lattice, and at a dihedral the two panels' tensions add instead of cancelling. An unbraced rim of this section carries only atm, less than half what the cell holds. So every face is braced in its own plane: the squares by vertex ties, the hexagons by six radial spokes each.

Bracing pays twice. Bending falls as the cube of the bracing pitch, so spokes buy strength cheaply — and they buy back volume. Every panel dimples inward and that dent is open to the sky, so an unbraced cell forfeits % of its own displacement. Spoked and solved over every real panel, the pumped-down article gives up % — the ledger carries it. On anything meant to float, that is a fifth of the lift.

Every member is bought; every joint is printed solid. Tube at × Euler margin even pinned, and joints in Bambu PAHT-CF (chopped-carbon nylon, kg/m³) weighed from their own geometry rather than budgeted. Solid because these parts sit inside the vacuum, where sparse infill is a virtual leak. The parts button takes the tubes away.

And it can assemble itself — press assemble, top centre. The build the animation plays is not choreography: it is the assembly prover's own order — inside-out, constrained so that every joint arrives carried by its own tree member — and every approach is a line the prover verified. The seed joint is set down first; after it, each of the members flies in along its proven straight-line escape, reversed, and every other joint of the rides in with its tree member, joint and pipe sliding home as one along the parent axis — the free-motion proof's own sweep, which is the only way a long spigot can engage at all. A closing member — there is no other way into a loop of fixed joints — arrives tilted at its own computed entry angle and rotates down onto its pilots. Every flight is swept against everything already seated at its moment, and the step buttons walk it one part at a time with each part's name, cut and joints — an assembly guide, not a film. The skin stays off until the frame is whole, because that is the build order too.

Float is decided by size, then the tube's grade — printing does not decide it. To float this cell would have to weigh under g — the air it displaces. It weighs kg, and that mass is kg of bought tube against kg of printed joints. The tube is nearly three quarters of it. Even with joints of zero mass this cell is an order of magnitude over.

Size first, then the grade. The budget is mass per cubic metre enclosed. Volume grows as the cube of the span, while the frame grows more slowly. A larger cell of the same architecture is lighter per litre. Two things follow. A hexagon face at this span contains no lattice point of its own, which is why it needs the rim, spokes and ties in view. One size up the face is full of them and that apparatus goes away. With M60J-class tube instead of the T700-class tube used here, the same geometry reaches kg/m³. It is buoyant in sea-level air as a single cell; T700 requires levels of frame inside frame.

This is the bench article, and the order it is built in is the

test: baked out under vacuum, the barrier bonded hot while the cell is already in the chamber, and the seal closed as the last operation before it cools. The chamber evacuates the cell. No valve or pump remains aboard, and nothing is pumped down afterwards. The crush comes last and it is not a step anyone performs: the cell comes out of the chamber and one atmosphere stands on it.

The same geometry printed end to end in nylon, the variant that proves the printer chain rather than the physics, comes to kg against g of displaced air.

The skin is hidden on this level, on purpose. The walk parks the camera at a joint inside the article, and a shell around that vantage is a wall, not a reading. Every other level keeps whatever skin mode the button holds.

These are the only designed parts in the airship. Everything else is bought: stock tube, stock film. Every joint grows from one signed distance field: spigots blended into a core, bores and an annular seating slot cut afterwards, the flat mating lands truncated last so no blend can round one off. Change a diameter and all regrow.

Capture is internal, and it grips three ways at once. The pipe slides over a hollow spigot, a collar closes outside it, and both bottom on a shoulder. The bore is the mandrel-formed face of the tube, the roundest surface it has. The spigot indexes there and the compliance lives outside. MPa on the glue line, and the spigot's own section carries MPa.

The nozzle no longer sets the size of the article — it sets the size of these. When the members were printed too, a mm bead forced every dimension above it: wall, strut, cell. Buying the tube broke that chain. What the bead still governs is the joinery — perimeters of it make the mm wall these parts are built from. They print solid, and not for strength: they live inside the vacuum, where sparse infill is a virtual leak.

Z is the governing direction, so every figure here uses the weak one. A printed part is far weaker between its layers than along them: MPa interlayer against MPa in plane. A pressure vessel is loaded in every direction at once, so this page quotes the interlayer number throughout, never the datasheet's lead line.

× the orthotropy toll

local buckling rides √(Eaxial·Ehoop), not the datasheet number

Roll-wrapped, not pultruded, and this is not a preference. A composite tube wall is fibres pointing somewhere, not one material. Local buckling of an orthotropic tube rides √(Ex·Eθ), so the governing modulus is Ex^¾·Eθ^¼. The best [0/90] split, three quarters axial and one quarter hoop, delivers of the fibre's headline stiffness. That factor has been in this model since the day the orthotropic penalty was written. A pultrusion is all-axial fibre: buying one would contradict the physics the article is priced by, and it would split at the sockets, where a spigot presses outward on hoop fibre a pultrusion does not have. Specify a [0/±45/90] wall, roll-wrapped over a mandrel. The bore is then the mandrel-formed surface, which is why the spigot indexes on the bore and the compliance lives outside it. The model prices the tube as .

What the tube is asked for. N per strut, × on Euler even pinned, × socketed, × on stress. And the published Euler figure is the conservative one twice over: the model takes the whole centre-to-centre length as the free length, but the member is held at its shoulders, over the mm cut. On that length it is × — a factor of better, and not claimed anywhere the model is used.

The wall thickness is set by the co-critical rule: thin enough that local buckling and Euler arrive together, thick enough that somebody can wrap it. That proportion fixes the wall as a constant fraction of strut length. The printer's thinnest reliable wall set the cell size before a single member was purchased, and the whole chain still runs backwards from the nozzle:

co-critical here is R/t · the flight reference optimises to R/t · the tube we buy is R/t

At floating densities struts are so slender that Euler buckling governs, not strength — solid rods give φ^ scaling and nothing floats. Hollow tubes restore φ^, if they are proportioned so that the strut's Euler load and the wall's local buckling arrive together. A catalogue section is not that proportion, and this page will not pretend otherwise: at R/t the bought tube is far inside co-critical, so local buckling is nowhere near it and the wall is carrying mass it does not need. That is the right trade at demonstrator scale — a stock section beats an optimal one you cannot order — and it is a trade the flight article cannot make. The proportion, not the topology, is what the mass turns on. And this wall is where the next rung lives: make it a lattice — two thin skins on a micro-truss core — and the exponent moves from to , the difference between and kg/m³ (× over the wall).

The material is bought by the metre. Dyneema composite fabric: a UHMWPE fibre grid laminated between films, the same stock as ultralight tents.

A panel pulls on the frame, not the lattice. Each one bulges inward until its membrane tension balances the atmosphere, then stands at that angle to the face plane. That is the load the rim and the spokes were sized against, and the reason the boundary needed a heavier tube than the interior.

Film mass scales with the span it bulges across, which is the whole reason the spokes exist: cut a hexagon into six triangles and each panel spans a fraction of what the bare face did. It is also why no cell size makes the film term go away: the areal mass falls exactly as fast as area-per-volume does.

The fabric is sized for strength, not for holding vacuum. Holding a vacuum for years is a different requirement: the film is a membrane, not a barrier, and a real barrier laminate of the measured class is several times heavier. Two things remain open: how the barrier is applied without cracking on first pump-down, and how the seams close. See the panels below.

The mating land

Where the film is supposed to be bonded, and where cells seat face to face. of the printed joints carry one or more: on a square centre, on a hub, at a corner where one square and two hexagons meet. flat faces in all, truncated last in the SDF so no blend can round one back off.

A hub collects N of a hexagon's load and hands it to three props at N each. The printed pad the design once carried is gone, and the manifest ships pad_r = . The arithmetic is why. A disc only picks up what the film hands it around its own perimeter, so at mm across it could collect N. It would have had to be mm across, wider than the hexagon, to take the hub's share. The spokes carry it. A pad was only ever local protection against a point support puncturing a film microns thick.

The loaded shape is solved and the net is formed to it. Three items belong to one coupon campaign — the seal-and-barrier coupons: the barrier stack, the forming process, and the bond.

It is a membrane, not a barrier. The g/m² above is sized for strength. A real barrier laminate of the class this project has measured is several times heavier, and wrapped around a cell this size that is lift-negative on its own skin — deliberately, because the demonstrator is not meant to be flight representative. Only a deposited stack scales to every cell size, and nobody has a measured number for one on printed PAHT-CF. That is the barrier-stack experiment, and it must be measured rather than argued. The arithmetic behind that comparison lives in research/notes/barrier-and-permeation.md, marked as arithmetic and under no checker, so this page does not put a figure on it.

The bulge is solved, and the net is formed to it. Every load number above takes the panel's dimple as of its half-span; that operating point sets the tension. The solved surface now exists at it: deeper at the centre than the cap idealization, and pressed rather than stretched. A flat sheet would need % strain; the fibre gives %. The same strain would craze any inorganic barrier coating on first pump-down. What remains genuinely open is the forming process itself: pressing a Dyneema-class laminate to a permanent crown without crazing the barrier stack is a coupon question, and it joins the seal-and-barrier campaign rather than being argued here.

The bond is undesigned, as the land card says. And Dyneema creeps under permanent load, so the spec is a heat-treated rigging grade or a Vectran laminate instead.

The structure is sized, gated and buildable; the skin is a fabric problem behind it, scoped to an experiment rather than argued on a page.
