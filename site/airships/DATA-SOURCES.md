## Invented exercise — `data/exercise/`

`exercise.json` is a synthetic exercise, not an agency record or a day of the season.
Every fire is named “Exercise NNN” and numbered `EXNNN`. Positions, sizes and outlines
are generated; dates, causes, incident URLs and real fire names are absent. Its provenance
sidecar records seed 7, the northern interior region (longitude −125.5 to −121.5,
latitude 57.0 to 59.8), source digests, fitted distributions and achieved clearances.

`tools/gen_exercise.py` fits the captured days at or above the 75th percentile of active
counts: 2026-08-12, 2026-08-14 and 2026-08-15. Count and size use type-7 quantiles; stage
uses pooled proportions. “Fire of Note” is not a control stage: its rows inform count and
size but are excluded from the stage fit. Draws interpolate quantiles, never sample an
incident. Larger outlines are area-scaled, sinusoidally perturbed 32-vertex polygons.

The generator rejects footprints outside the bundled provincial outline, over bundled
water, within 150 km of any guard entry or season fire of note, or within 25 km of a
listed community. It applies a further 45 km community clearance for placement so the
model's 40 km community-naming logic cannot name a real community in the exercise.
Historical outlines of those fires are enclosed conservatively for these distance checks.
Fires on neither list reject nothing: the sidecar records how near the nearest captured
outline of one comes to an exercise footprint (`nearestOtherFire`). These are
checks against the repository's dated data and named community list, not a complete
settlement inventory or a survey of terrain. The terrain image is a hillshade, not an
altitude field. Real water comes from `water-bc.json`; no wind or satellite heat is invented.

The generated files use the project's Apache-2.0 licence. The statistical inputs remain
under OGL–British Columbia; bundled outline, terrain and lake attribution and limitations
remain as documented above. No source is fetched by the generator or gate. Regenerate with
`python3 tools/gen_exercise.py`; verify with `make exercisecheck`.

## 9. BC Wildfire Service — the 2026 season record and its status days

**What it is.** `data/season/`: British Columbia's 2026 wildfire season as data, for a
labelled replay of the season on the monitor page. `2026.json` holds one record for each of
the 1,439 fires in the BC Wildfire Service's public incidents layer when a complete copy of
it was taken at 2026-10-02T02:01:28Z. `days/<date>/fires.json` and `perims.json` hold the
status days: for each date that has one, the last dated copy of the public record as it
stood that day, every fire that was not out. `2026.summary.json` and `2026.days.json`
are computed from those, and `2026.prov.json` is the provenance record for all of them. The
shapes, the rule that keeps hindsight away from the dispatch model and the policy on
impossible dates are in [`data/README.md`](data/README.md).

**Publisher.** Province of British Columbia, BC Wildfire Service (Ministry of Forests).

**Licence.** Open Government Licence – British Columbia, version 2.0.
<https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/data/open-data/open-government-licence-bc>

**Required attribution**, verbatim as the licence specifies it where the provider gives no
statement of its own:

> Contains information licensed under the Open Government Licence – British Columbia.

**Where it came from.** The two public layers of section 1, by three routes. None of them
is a request made by `pipeline/season.py`, which opens no network connection.

- A complete, unfiltered copy of both layers (`where=1=1`, every field), taken once at
  2026-10-02T02:01:28Z: two pages of incidents and one of perimeters, with each layer's own
  count and status tally taken alongside to show the copy is complete. It is the source of
  `2026.json` and of the status day 2026-10-01.
- 113 dated copies each of the live mirror's `fires.json` and `perims.json`, written by
  `pipeline/live.py` between 2026-08-10T04:14:51Z and 2026-09-22T20:02:56Z and recovered
  from this site's deploy history. The last copy of each local date is the status day for
  2026-08-09 to 2026-08-15, 2026-09-20 and 2026-09-22.
- `data/snapshot.json` (section 1), which is the status day 2026-08-08.

The raw copies run to megabytes and are not in the repository. `data/season/2026.prov.json`
lists each of them under `inputs` by sha256 and size.

**How it was processed.** The Information was modified.

- `2026.json`: the incidents layer reduced to sixteen fields per fire; epoch-millisecond
  dates converted to America/Vancouver calendar dates, with the published values kept
  beside them; fires sorted by the sequence number in the fire number; ten fires whose
  dates cannot be true flagged, kept and counted, and left off the day-by-day timeline
  unless a stated rule places them.
- Status days from the mirror history: nothing. Each file is the recovered copy, byte for
  byte. The mirror's own request filters to status not `Out` and has the server generalise
  perimeters by `maxAllowableOffset=0.002`, as section 1 describes.
- The status day from the snapshot: `FIRE_CENTRE` dropped from each fire and `FIRE_URL`
  from each perimeter.
- The status day from the complete copy: filtered to status not `Out`, reduced to the
  mirror's property names, perimeters generalised here to 0.002 degrees and rounded to
  four decimal places.

replay built on these files or any use made of them, and nothing here has official status.

**Not for emergency use.** These are dated copies of a public record. The newest is of
1 October 2026 and none of them is updated. For a real fire, use the BC Wildfire Service:
<https://wildfiresituation.nrs.gov.bc.ca/>.

**Load discipline.** `pipeline/season.py` and its gate make no request to anyone. The
complete copy cost the service nine requests, which its manifest lists; the mirror copies
were already being taken for the live page.

**Three things to be honest about.** `2026.json` is hindsight in every field, the ignition
date included, and the size a fire reached must never be shown to anything that replays a
decision made on an earlier day. The status days are few: on every other date of the
season there is no dated record of what was known, and 1,207 of the 1,439 fires are on no
status day at all. And the record itself is imperfect: ten fires carry dates that cannot
be true, the publisher revises ignition dates after the fact, and at least one fire was
renumbered between a status day and the complete copy.

## 10. The monitor's no-fleet guard — `data/season/2026.guard.json`

The last file in `data/season/` is not agency data at all. `2026.guard.json` is this
project's own editorial ruling, written by hand: the days no fleet is simulated for
(8 to 27 August 2026, the provincial state of emergency), the 22 fires the simulated fleet
never works — each with its tier and its basis: a loss, an evacuation order, an alert, or
first discovery near people — two places kept clear on their own dates, and the default
keep-out distance. Every entry carries exactly one source link to the public reporting it
rests on. The file quotes nothing and names nobody, and `2026.guard.prov.json` records
that it was written by hand rather than generated.

**Licence.** Apache-2.0, the project's own code licence: this is an editorial file by this
project, not a copy of anyone's data. The linked sources remain their publishers' own.

**Why it is a data file.** The rule it encodes — never replay a tragedy with a better
ending — is enforced in code (`sim/guard.js`) but carried as data, so that widening a
window or softening a distance is an edit anyone can see. `tests/guard/` pins the file's
canonical digest, which makes that edit a deliberate two-place change, and the monitor
stands its fleet down rather than flying when the file cannot be read.
